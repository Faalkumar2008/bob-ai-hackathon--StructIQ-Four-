# Problem Statement
## Bridge Health Monitoring & Predictive Maintenance

---

## The Challenge

Bridges are among the most critical components of civil infrastructure. Globally,
hundreds of thousands of bridges carry billions of vehicle crossings every year.
Yet the systems used to monitor their health have not kept pace with the scale of
the problem.

### Key Statistics

| Metric | Figure |
|--------|--------|
| US bridges classified structurally deficient | 7.5% (~42,000 bridges) |
| US bridges over 50 years old | 42% |
| Annual US bridge maintenance backlog | $123 billion |
| Average gap between manual inspections | 2 years |
| Real-time early-warning systems deployed at scale | Near zero |

### Root Causes

**1. Inspection Frequency**
Regulatory frameworks in most countries require visual bridge inspections every
2 years. In that window, progressive structural degradation — fatigue crack
propagation, rebar corrosion, bearing failure, settlement — can advance
significantly without detection.

**2. Subjective Assessment**
Visual inspections are highly dependent on the inspector's experience and
conditions on the day. Sub-surface defects (internal delamination, hidden
corrosion, embedded crack growth) are invisible to the naked eye.

**3. No Continuous Data**
Without sensor networks, engineers have no data between inspection cycles.
They cannot detect sudden load events, progressive deflection, or resonance
anomalies in real time.

**4. Reactive Maintenance Model**
Most bridge maintenance is reactive — damage is only addressed once it becomes
visible or a failure has occurred. Reactive maintenance is 3–5× more expensive
than preventive maintenance.

**5. No Predictive Capability**
Without continuous data, it is impossible to accurately forecast when a component
will reach end-of-life. Budget allocation is based on age and visual condition
rather than measured structural performance.

### The Human Cost

The consequences of inadequate bridge monitoring are not merely financial:

- **I-35W Mississippi River Bridge collapse (Minneapolis, 2007):** 13 killed,
  145 injured. Post-collapse analysis identified steel gusset plate fatigue —
  a condition that continuous strain monitoring would have flagged years earlier.
- **Fern Hollow Bridge collapse (Pittsburgh, 2022):** No injuries, but the bridge
  had been rated "poor" for 5 years with no action taken.
- **Morandi Bridge collapse (Genoa, Italy, 2018):** 43 killed. Cable corrosion
  and fatigue were identifiable through sensor-based monitoring.

### Who Has This Problem?

| Stakeholder | Pain Point |
|-------------|-----------|
| Bridge engineers | No real-time structural data between inspections |
| Asset managers | Cannot prioritise maintenance budget based on actual risk |
| Transportation agencies | Reactive repairs cost 3–5× more than planned maintenance |
| Government / public | Bridge failures cause loss of life and economic disruption |
| Insurance / risk | Cannot quantify structural risk without continuous monitoring data |

### Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

- **Manual inspections:** Infrequent, subjective, cannot detect sub-surface defects
- **Periodic NDT surveys:** Expensive, still point-in-time, require bridge closure
- **Existing SHM systems:** Expensive, proprietary, no AI layer, no natural language interface
- **No AI integration:** Engineers cannot query structural data conversationally

---

## Summary

The core problem is a **data gap**: bridges deteriorate continuously, but are
only assessed periodically. This gap creates the conditions for preventable
failures. The solution requires **continuous sensing**, **intelligent analysis**,
and **accessible reporting** — delivered through an AI-powered platform that
engineers can interact with naturally.

> "We don't need more inspections. We need continuous intelligence."
